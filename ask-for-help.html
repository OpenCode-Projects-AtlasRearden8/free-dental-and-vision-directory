A Kind Ask-for-Help ScriptOne page · print this

Asking for help is a skill, not a character flaw. This page is designed to be printed on one sheet and kept by the phone. Fill the blanks once; reuse it forever.

Before you dial (2 minutes)

  • Write your one-sentence need: “I need a free or cheap cleaning / eye exam / hearing-aid screening.”
  • Have two times this week you could actually go. Real dates are magic.
  • Set the papers from what to bring beside the phone.

The script

Hello, my name is             . I'm looking for help affording   dental / vision / hearing   care. I don't currently have  insurance / a dentist in network / money for a hearing aid .

Could you tell me:

1) what free or low-cost options exist near  ZIP code  (name city/town if 211 asks) ?

2) whether those options are currently taking new people ?

3) what paperwork those places will want from me ?

And could I ask the names and phone numbers of the best two places to try today?

Thank you so much — you've genuinely helped. Could I have your name, in case I need to call back?

That's the whole call. Usually 3–6 minutes. You only need the first two questions if you're overwhelmed; stopping politely after them is fine.

If the first person can't help

  1. Say: “Thank you anyway. Could you point me to one place or one person who might know more?” Bouncing once from one person to another is normal and counts as progress.
  2. If you hit a wall entirely, end kindly and move on to the next resource on the Dental / Vision / Hearing page.
  3. Write down every name + phone number. Persistence, not luck, is how help is found — and it's how most people do find it.

Words that open doors

  • “Could you walk me through today's appointment cost range?” vs. “How much will it cost?” (signals a real consumer, gets real answers)
  • “I'm not sure I qualify — could I still come and check?”
  • “This is my first time doing this — could you take that into account?”
  • “Do you have a sliding scale, a payment plan, or know who does?”
  • “Is there a waiting list, and am I on it?”

Words to avoid

  • “Free anything unlimited.” Asking for more than you need can slow down eligibility nurses, not help.
  • Apologizing everywhere. A short “I appreciate you” goes further than three “I'm sorry”s.
  • Explaining your whole story. Program people hear a lot of stories; a short summary plus direct questions gets you helped faster.
  • Giving someone your SSN or bank details out loud in a first call. Reputable programs ask for paperwork in person or by secure form — verify who you're talking to first.
If focusing, one phone call at a time is already enough today. A call tomorrow from the same list counts as a second call, not a failure.
