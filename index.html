Start where your body hurts

Teeth, eyes, and ears are the three areas health insurance most often leaves behind. If you're hurting right now, pick the page that matches and go. Everything here is written for a tired reader at the end of a long day.

  • Dental help — dental-school clinics with sliding-scale fees, community health centers, and free dental days.
  • Vision help — programs that give free eye exams or vouchers toward glasses.
  • Hearing help — charities, recycled hearing-aid programs, and fitting help.
  • Free clinic-day events — big one- and two-day events where no one is turned away for money.

The two quickest first steps for almost anyone

  1. Call 211. Dial 211 on any phone, or visit 211.org. It's a free, nationwide information line for exactly this kind of help: dental clinics, glasses vouchers, hearing programs, and more, for your specific town. Ask: “Can you give me a list of free or low-cost dental/vision/hearing clinics near me?”
  2. Find your nearest community health center. Use the federal lookup tool at findahealthcenter.hrsa.gov — it lists clinics that charge on a sliding scale (fees based on your income, with some patients paying little or nothing). Many also offer dental care on the same visit.

These two steps work for nearly everyone in the U.S., so they're the backbone of this whole directory. The other pages add specialty programs on top.

How to use this directory in ten minutes

  1. Pick your area: Dental, Vision, or Hearing.
  2. Call 211 with your question ready (the ask-for-help script gives you the exact words).
  3. Check the free clinic-day events page — if a big event is coming near you, one trip may handle everything.
  4. Before you go, gather the papers listed on What to bring. It makes your first visit dramatically smoother.
Be careful of look-alikes. Genuine programs don't ask you to pay a “processing fee” to apply, don't call you about a prize connected to your exam, and don't demand gift cards. If a website asks for sensitive information before telling you anything about the program, stop and call a verified number instead (211, or the program's own listed phone).
