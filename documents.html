What to Bring, First VisitChecklist

Every free and sliding-scale clinic asks for roughly the same little stack of papers. Bring it all once and every program becomes easier. Don't have everything? Go anyway — most clinics will help you fill gaps; call ahead and say so.

Education, not medical advice. Every clinic varies — call ahead and ask, “Is there paperwork I should bring to my first visit?” This checklist covers what they almost always mention.

The core stack

  • ☐ Photo ID — driver's license, state ID, passport, or consular ID. One for each adult; school ID can work for teens.
  • ☐ Proof of address — a recent utility bill, lease, or piece of mail with your name.
  • ☐ Your Social Security or ITIN card/number (often for applications, not always required — call first). Programs that need it will say so; you rarely need the card itself.
  • ☐ Income proof (last 1–2 pay stubs, benefit award letters, tax return (most recent)). Most sliding scales compute fees from income; some just use “about this much” numbers.
  • ☐ Any insurance you already have — Medicaid card, Medicare card, VA card, private card. Bring it even if you're sure it doesn't cover this program; sometimes it partially does.
  • ☐ Medication list — every medication, the dose, and what pharmacy you use. Bottle photos count. Include over-the-counter pills, inhalers, and vitamins.
  • ☐ Allergies list — especially antibiotic allergies before dental visits, or latex.
  • ☐ Medical history you know — any hospital stays, surgeries, or ongoing conditions. Bullet points on the back of an envelope count.
  • ☐ Your current glasses, hearing aids, or dentures — providers measure improvement from these.
  • ☐ Previous records, if you have them — old x-rays, audiograms, prescriptions. Not required, genuinely helpful.
  • ☐ The payment you might owe — some sliding scales still want a small fee (often $10–$40) the same day. Ask what the minimum is when you book.

Six things to ask when you call to book

  1. “Do you take new patients right now, and how long is the wait?”
  2. “What exact papers should I bring for the fee tier before my visit?”
  3. “What's the lowest fee for someone with no income, if I qualify?”
  4. “Do you need anything specific before I come in — like prior x-rays or an interpreter request?”
  5. “What's your cancellation policy? Is there a no-show fee?”
  6. “Do you have language interpreters or mobility accommodations?” (Ask. This is standard care, not a bother.)

If you're missing a document

  • No ID? Ask the clinic what alternative they accept — many accept affidavits or can work around it at first visits.
  • No income proof? Any simple written note from a shelter, church, employer, or helper agency about your situation is often enough at first visits.
  • No fixed address? Many sliding-scale clinics deliberately serve people without paperwork. Say so plainly, first sentence of the conversation.
